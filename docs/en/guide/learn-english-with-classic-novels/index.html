Learning English with classic novels: which book and which level to start with

Most English learners reach a point where they want to read a real book. Classic novels are a good goal: the stories are well known, there are films of them, and the authors' own sentences have been read for generations. The hard part is the first page, with its old-fashioned words and long sentences. The answer is to read the classic at your level first and move towards the full text over time.

Start with a short book

Your first book should be short enough to finish. A finished book motivates you far more than a long novel left halfway. These books on Livrino are good first reads in English:

  • The Canterville Ghost (Oscar Wilde): short, funny and full of dialogue.
  • Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (R. L. Stevenson): a gripping mystery in short episodes.
  • The Hound of the Baskervilles (Arthur Conan Doyle): Sherlock Holmes's most famous case, a little longer.

Choose your level: A2, B1 or full text

A2 texts use short sentences and everyday words; B1 adds longer sentences and more detail; the full text is the author's own words. Starting one step below your level lets you enjoy the first episodes; move up when it flows. Not sure of your level? See What do A2 and B1 mean?

Listen as you read

Seeing and hearing the same sentence trains pronunciation and listening together. Play a balloon, then say it aloud yourself. One episode a day is enough to build the habit.

Compare with your language, but not every line

Checking the translation of a sentence you can't crack helps; checking every sentence stops you thinking in English. Look at the picture and the context first, guess, then compare if you need to.
